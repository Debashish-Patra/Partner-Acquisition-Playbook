# Partner and franchise acquisition playbook: SEO and paid media

A practical template for companies that grow through independent partners, franchisees or agents, and want to recruit them online. It covers the funnel, the page, the channels, the bidding path, measurement, decision rules and compliance.

Status: template. Every number in this playbook is either a formula or an illustrative assumption. Replace the assumptions with your own baseline after the first 30 days of data.

## 1. Who this is for

Use it when all of these are true:

- You recruit partners who bring you customers, for example franchisees, agents, distributors, advisors or resellers.
- A partner needs screening, documents or a licence before they can start.
- The partner's value shows up over months, not on the day they sign.

If a partner can sign up and start earning in one step, most of the screening and value sections below can be simplified.

## 2. The funnel and the events that matter

The most common mistake in partner acquisition is optimising for form fills. A form fill is cheap and tells you little. Define every stage in writing, agree the definitions between marketing and sales, and record each stage as an event in your CRM.

| Stage | Event | Recorded by | How it is used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visit | Landing page view | Analytics | Audiences and remarketing pools |
| Lead | Registration form submitted | Form and CRM | Early optimisation event while volume is low |
| Qualified | Passes a screening call: eligible, serious, in a target geography | Sales or telecalling, logged in CRM | Main bidding event once volume allows |
| Approved | Documents verified and onboarding approved | Operations or compliance, logged in CRM | Second bidding event, imported from the CRM |
| Activated | First customer onboarded or first revenue within a set number of days | Business team | Value signal for value-based bidding and the best seed for lookalikes |
| Retained | Still active at 6 or 12 months | Business team | Analysis and partner quality scoring, not bidding |

Write down three things for each stage: the exact definition, the maximum time allowed to reach it, and the person who owns it.

## 3. Readiness checklist before any spend

Paid media amplifies whatever is behind it. If a lead waits three days for a call, more spend only produces more waiting leads.

- A landing page that loads in under three seconds on mobile and has one clear call to action.
- A lead form with qualification questions (see section 6.1).
- A defined first response time. A starting target is a call or message within one business hour, with an automatic confirmation message at once.
- CRM integration so every lead carries its source, campaign and keyword.
- Tracking that records each funnel stage as an event, tested end to end with a real lead.
- A claims library: every statement in an ad or on the page has a source and an approver.
- Creative assets approved by compliance before launch.
- Support capacity: a named person for onboarding and a plan for the first 30 days of a new partner. Partners who sign and then get no help do not activate, and your cost per activated partner rises.

## 4. SEO workstream

SEO is slow and compounding. Expect three to six months before it contributes meaningfully, so judge it on leading indicators in the first quarter.

### 4.1 Phases

1. Competitor and gap analysis. Compare five to ten competing partner programmes on page structure, content depth, keyword coverage, internal linking, schema, trust elements, lead flow and calculators. The output is a ranked list of gaps with an effort estimate, not a general report.
2. Landing page rebuild. Make one page the hub for all partner searches and give it a single clear topic.
3. Supporting content. Build educational pages around the questions prospects ask before they register.
4. Authority building. Earn links and mentions from relevant publications, industry bodies and partner communities.
5. Measurement and iteration. Review rankings, indexation and leads by page every month.

### 4.2 Landing page structure

- A hero with a title that matches the search intent and one primary button.
- Trust metrics, but only ones you can source and keep current.
- Value cards for what the partner gets: economics, products, support, marketing help, technology.
- A short explanation of who the partner is and how the model works, in 100 to 150 words.
- A three-step registration journey: register, check eligibility, submit documents.
- An earnings explainer with assumptions and disclaimers. Prefer an illustrative range with the working shown over a free-form calculator, unless your compliance team has approved the calculator.
- Real partner stories, with measurable results where you can substantiate them.
- A 15 to 20 question FAQ marked up with FAQ schema.
- Links into your learning hub.
- A persistent but unobtrusive registration form.

### 4.3 Keywords by intent

| Tier | Intent | Example pattern | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ready to sign up | become a [partner type], [partner type] registration | Landing page |
| 2 | Comparing or checking | [partner type] earnings, eligibility, documents required | Supporting pages and FAQs |
| 3 | Learning | how to build a [business type], certification guide | Blog and learning hub |
| 4 | Brand and competitor | [your brand] partner, [competitor] partner | Landing page and separate paid campaign |

Add city-level pages if local presence matters, for example "[partner type] in [city]". Give each page unique content. Thin pages that only swap the city name hurt more than they help.

### 4.4 Monthly cadence

| Activity | Starting volume | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Educational posts | 4 to 6 a month | Tier 2 and 3 coverage |
| Internal links from existing site pages | 15 to 20 at launch, then as new posts go live | Passes authority to the hub |
| Off-page links and mentions | 10 to 20 a month, quality over count | Authority |
| Technical check | Monthly | Speed, indexation, schema errors |
| Ranking review | Monthly, 20 core keywords | Trend by tier |

### 4.5 SEO measures

Organic sessions, rankings for the core keywords, indexed pages, landing page conversion rate, organic leads, and organic qualified leads. The last one matters most, because organic traffic can look healthy while producing weak leads.

## 5. Paid media workstream

### 5.1 Channel roles

| Channel | Job | Typical campaign types |
|---|---|---|
| Search | Capture people already looking for a partnership | Generic intent, brand, competitor (see section 9) |
| Social lead ads | Create demand among people who fit the profile but are not searching | Lookalike, interest and profession targeting, remarketing |
| Display and video | Build awareness and bring visitors back | Remarketing, in-market audiences |

### 5.2 Starting budget split

Use this as a starting point and rebalance with the rules in section 7.

| Channel | Starting share |
|---|---|
| Search | 50 to 60% |
| Social lead ads | 20 to 30% |
| Display, video and remarketing | 10 to 15% |
| Test reserve | About 10% |

Search gets the largest share because intent is highest there, but its inventory is limited. When search impression share on your core keywords is already high, extra money belongs elsewhere.

### 5.3 Bidding ladder

Move up the ladder only when the data supports it. Platform thresholds change, so check current guidance. The conversion counts below are rules of thumb.

| Step | Optimise for | Move on when |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Lead (form submit), with a form that has qualification questions | Weeks 1 to 4, while data builds |
| 1 | Target cost per qualified lead, with qualified leads imported from the CRM | About 30 or more qualified leads a month |
| 2 | Approved partner as a second conversion, or value-based bidding with a value per partner tier | About 50 or more valued conversions in a month, and a stable value model |

Why this matters: at step 0 the platform finds people who fill forms, which includes many who will never qualify. Importing qualified and approved events from the CRM teaches the platform what a good partner looks like. This is the largest single quality lever in the playbook.

### 5.4 Search structure

- Separate campaigns by intent tier, so budget and bids follow value.
- Keep brand terms in their own campaign so they do not hide generic performance.
- Add negative keywords from the first week: jobs, salary, free, login, complaints, and any term that signals customers, not partners.
- Write ad copy that screens out the wrong audience, for example by stating eligibility.

### 5.5 Social lead ads

- Use the higher-intent form type where available, with a review step before submission. It cuts volume and raises quality.
- Add two or three qualification questions to the form (section 6.1).
- Build lookalikes from the best seed you have, in this order: activated partners, approved partners, qualified leads. Avoid seeding from all form fills.
- Check that the seed has enough people for the platform to model. If it does not, use interest and profession targeting first and build the seed over time.
- Send qualified and approved events back through the platform's server-side conversion interface when your setup allows.

### 5.6 Creatives

Test one variable at a time: the value proposition, the proof point, the audience framing or the format. Keep a creative log with the claim, approver and approval date.

## 6. Lead qualification and handoff

### 6.1 Form questions

Ask only what you need to qualify. Suggested fields:

- Name, mobile number, email
- City and state
- Current occupation
- Relevant certification or licence, yes or no
- Existing client or customer base, in bands
- Time they can commit
- Readiness to invest, in bands, if there is a start-up cost

### 6.2 Simple lead score

Give each lead 0 to 100 from the answers above, using weights that reflect what predicts activation in your own history. Review the weights quarterly against the retained partners. Use the score to route: high scores get a call first, low scores get an automated nurture sequence.

### 6.3 Response and follow-up

| Step | Target |
|---|---|
| Automatic confirmation | Immediately |
| First call attempt | Within one business hour |
| Follow-up attempts | At least five over seven days, on different times and channels |
| Nurture for the unready | Monthly content for six months |

Log the reason for every lead that drops out. Over a quarter, those reasons tell you what to fix in targeting, copy or the offer.

## 7. Measurement and decision rules

### 7.1 KPI tree

Spend, then clicks (click-through rate, cost per click), then leads (conversion rate, cost per lead), then qualified leads (qualification rate, cost per qualified lead), then approved partners (approval rate, cost per approved partner), then activated partners (activation rate, cost per activated partner), then partner revenue and payback.

The measure that decides budget is cost per activated partner against partner value, not cost per lead.

### 7.2 Tracking

Every campaign needs a UTM scheme and a CRM field for each parameter.

| Parameter | Convention |
|---|---|
| utm_source | google, meta, organic, referral |
| utm_medium | cpc, paid-social, organic, email |
| utm_campaign | [market]-[tier]-[objective]-[yyyymm] |
| utm_content | Creative identifier |
| utm_term | Keyword, for search |

Reconcile three sources every week: the ad platforms, web analytics and the CRM. If they disagree by more than about 10%, fix tracking before changing any bids.

### 7.3 Worked example with assumed numbers

All inputs below are assumptions for illustration. Replace them with your own.

| Input | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Cost per lead | 100 |
| Lead to qualified | 30% |
| Qualified to approved | 20% |
| Approved to activated | 60% |

Cost per qualified lead = 100 / 0.30 = 333.
Cost per approved partner = 100 / (0.30 x 0.20) = 1,667.
Cost per activated partner = 1,667 / 0.60 = 2,778.

If an activated partner contributes a margin of 1,000 a month and stays active for 12 months, the contribution is 12,000, which is about 4.3 times the acquisition cost. If your own numbers give a ratio below about 3, or a payback longer than your tolerance, fix the weakest conversion step before you add spend.

### 7.4 Targets

Do not set targets in the first 30 days. Measure the baseline for each stage, then set targets as a range for each step, a floor you must hold and a goal you aim for. Review them every quarter.

### 7.5 Decision rules

These are starting heuristics to adjust with experience.

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| A campaign has spent about three times your target cost per qualified lead with no qualified leads | Pause it and review targeting and copy |
| A channel's cost per activated partner is 30% or more below the average for three weeks | Move 10 to 20% of the budget towards it, then wait one to two weeks before the next shift |
| A channel's cost per activated partner is 30% or more above the average for three weeks | Cut or restructure it |
| Lead volume is strong but qualification rate falls | Tighten the form, adjust audiences, or move one step up the bidding ladder |
| Qualified volume is steady but approval rate is low | Review the eligibility messaging and the documents list |
| Approval is high but activation is low | Review onboarding support, not media |
| Search impression share on core keywords is already high | Shift incremental budget to social or remarketing |

Do not make several changes at once. Change one thing, record it, and give it time before reading the result, normally one to two weeks of stable data.

## 8. Weekly review

Fifteen minutes, with marketing, sales and operations in the room.

1. Spend against plan, by channel.
2. Funnel numbers by stage, with week-on-week change.
3. Cost per qualified, approved and activated partner.
4. Lead response times and the stage with the biggest drop.
5. Tracking reconciliation.
6. Tests running, results and next decisions.
7. Compliance items: new claims, creatives awaiting approval.

## 9. Compliance and risk

Regulated businesses carry extra risk when they recruit partners, because the partner will later sell products to the public. Treat this section as a list of questions for your legal and compliance teams, not as legal advice.

- Income claims. Do not imply guaranteed or typical earnings unless you can substantiate them. Show assumptions and add a disclaimer. Check testimonials too: results of the best partners are not the typical result.
- Competitor names. Bidding on competitor names may be allowed by the platform and still raise trademark or misleading-advertising questions. Do not use competitor names in ad text without legal sign-off.
- Customer data. Uploading customer contact lists to advertising platforms for matching or lookalikes needs a lawful basis and usually consent. Prefer first-party partner and prospect data captured with clear consent. Hash identifiers, share the minimum, and set a retention limit.
- Outreach. Calls and messages must respect do-not-disturb rules and opt-in requirements for messaging apps.
- Terminology. Roles can be renamed by regulators. Content can mention an old term where people still search for it, but should explain the current official term.
- Disclosures. Add the sector regulator's required warnings to ads and pages.
- Approvals. Keep a log: claim, source, approver, date, expiry.

India notes (verify current rules before use): broker-side partners are regulated under the securities regulator's authorised person framework, which replaced the older sub-broker category. Financial advertising falls under the advertising standards council's guidelines and the securities regulator's rules for intermediaries. Personal data handling falls under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Telemarketing follows the telecom regulator's rules.

## 10. First 90 days

| Weeks | Focus | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Readiness | Definitions agreed, tracking tested, page and form live, claims approved |
| 3 to 6 | Search and SEO | Search campaigns on step 0, competitor gap report, page rebuilt, first posts published |
| 7 to 10 | Social and remarketing | Lead ads live, remarketing pools built, CRM events flowing back |
| 11 to 13 | Optimise | Baselines set, first targets set, move to step 1 if volume allows, review budget split |

## 11. Common failure modes

- Optimising for leads and judging success by cost per lead.
- Marketing and sales using different definitions of a qualified lead.
- Slow follow-up on fresh leads.
- No feedback loop from the CRM to the ad platforms.
- Lookalikes built from weak seeds.
- Judging SEO after 30 days.
- Treating signing as the finish line and neglecting the first 30 days of the partner's journey.
- Making many changes at the same time and learning nothing from any of them.
